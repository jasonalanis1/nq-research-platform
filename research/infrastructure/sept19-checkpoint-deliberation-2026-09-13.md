## Topic 1 — Research-first vs. build-now (the "two visions" Jason flagged)

**Claude's opening framing:** the project has quietly been running under one
default posture without ever stating it plainly — stay research-first,
don't build or ship anything real until a full directional edge clears
Discovery -> Validation -> Holdout on its own. Called this Vision A.
Named Vision B as the alternative: take what's already proven (the
validated state/conditioning facts) and build something real with it now,
even if partial, rather than waiting indefinitely for a standalone edge.

**Jason's response:** doesn't want to pick one exclusively. He likes the
research and wants it to continue. But he wants to actually paper-trade
with what's already been validated now, to see if it's even remotely
successful, and treat early results as the way to fund the next tier
(more data, etc.) rather than asking for budget up front. Explicit
caveat, in his own words: doesn't want to "lose what we've had" — the
goal is continue AND maintain, not a wholesale pivot away from the
existing base to chase something new.

His real emphasis, though, is on HOW the research itself works —
specifically hypothesis generation. Not that anything is currently wrong,
but: is the project only really capable of answering one question/testing
one lens at a time, when it could be asking several different questions
of the same data to surface more distinct strategy candidates? He wants
this — the actual mechanics of how ideas get generated and scanned — to
be the real deep-dive for the 19th, not a side note.

**Where this landed:** a blended posture, not A-or-B — two concurrent
tracks:
1. Upgrade the hypothesis-generation process itself: broaden the number
   of distinct angles/questions being asked of the data, not just refine
   the single-lens search that's been running.
2. Start paper trading with what's already validated, to get a real read
   on viability, with early results funding the next tier of investment
   rather than needing budget approved in advance.

**Open item Jason flagged specifically for the 19th:** he does not want to
arrive at that checkpoint and find the shelf still running dry at the
current idea-generation rate. The hypothesis-generation-methodology
question needs to be a real, scoped focus by then — not just acknowledged.

## Topic 2 — Conditional/regime-based re-testing of dead ideas (the "reverse level sweep" example)

**Claude's position:** agreed this is a real and legitimate question,
genuinely different from "does it work on average." The project's own
newly-validated state facts (range-contraction, overnight-coil, etc.)
are direct proof that conditioning on market state changes the answer —
a pattern with no edge in aggregate can still have a real edge inside a
specific, well-reasoned subset of conditions. Flagged the risk plainly:
this is also the easiest way to fool yourself if done wrong. The
difference between a legitimate conditional finding and overfitting is
discipline — pick ONE conditioning variable ahead of time, based on an
actual mechanism/reason (not by slicing the data until something looks
good), then test that specific claim through the normal Discovery-first,
Validation/Holdout-confirms process.

**Jason's response:** agreed it's worth exploring further, and pointed
out (correctly) that this is really one concrete instance of the Topic 1
question — "asking a different/better question of the same data" IS the
hypothesis-generation upgrade he wants. Repeated the same caveat as
Topic 1: wants to build on top of what's proven, not discard it while
chasing this.

**Where this landed:** folded into Topic 1 as a first concrete example of
the broader hypothesis-generation upgrade, rather than a separate side
quest — re-testing old, closed directional ideas (starting with level
sweep reversal) conditioned on the project's own proven state facts.
