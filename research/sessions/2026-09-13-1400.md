## What was tested
Two full pre-registered Discovery scans, both draws this cycle:
1. Entry 17/M13 -- leveraged-ETF sponsors (2x/3x funds) must rebalance
   their exposure into the closing bell every day, in the direction the
   market already moved, and that rebalancing should show up biggest in
   the instrument with the largest such-fund complex relative to its own
   market size (Russell 2000/RTY), smaller in the S&P 500 (ES), smallest
   in the Nasdaq-100 (NQ). Tested whether the last 30 minutes actually
   move further in the day's own direction, on unusually large days,
   across all three.
2. Entry 18/M14 -- options-dealer hedging: when dealers are net "short
   gamma" (positioned such that they must buy more as the market rises
   and sell more as it falls), that mandatory hedging should make the
   last half hour move further in whatever direction the day was already
   going, compared to when dealers are positioned the other way. Tested
   whether NQ and ES actually show a bigger late-day push on days that
   follow a stretch of dealer-short-gamma-like price behavior.

## Result and why it matters
Both came back clean nulls -- genuinely useful information, not a wasted
test. Two of this project's longest-standing "parked, need data" entries
were finally resolved this cycle, and both came back negative:
1. M13 (LETF rebalance): none of the three instruments showed the
   predicted late-day push, not even the one (RTY) where the effect was
   supposed to be strongest. This closes the leveraged-ETF-rebalance
   story for NQ, ES, and RTY alike -- a clean, well-powered null across
   ~4.25 years of data per instrument.
2. M14 (dealer gamma): neither NQ nor ES showed the predicted
   stronger-push effect in the higher-risk regime -- if anything, both
   pointed the wrong way (though not strongly enough to call it a real
   reversal effect either). This closes the dealer-hedging late-day
   story too, at least as measurable from price alone.
