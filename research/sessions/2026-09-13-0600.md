## What was tested
Scan 026 tested M18's full frozen scope: whether NQ's next-session RTH
return, sign-adjusted against today's RTH return, is more negative
(more reversal) after high-RTH-volume days than after low-volume days.
"Volume" here means today's RTH trading volume relative to its own
trailing 20-day average, split into thirds (low/mid/high), frozen on
the Discovery data only. All 10 pre-registered cells ran: the gating
cell, the low/mid comparison cells, the high-minus-low gap test, the
plain (unconditional) baseline, two cells separating genuine liquidity-
driven reversal from an "informed trader" confound flagged in the
literature, two cells checking whether the effect held up across the
first vs. second half of the Discovery period, and one kill-rule check
for whether the whole effect was really just a single opening-minute
print artifact.

## Result and why it matters
The headline gating test DID pass on its own: after high-volume days,
NQ's next-session return reversed by a credible margin (essentially,
tomorrow tended to partly give back today's move, and that pattern was
statistically solid, not noise). But the second, equally load-bearing
test failed: that same reversal was NOT credibly bigger after high-
volume days than after low-volume days -- both showed roughly the same
amount of reversal. In plain terms, NQ has some everyday mean-reversion
regardless of volume, and this test does not show that volume makes it
worse. The literature's specific claim -- that trading volume acts as a
dial that predicts HOW MUCH tomorrow will reverse -- is not supported
here. The kill check ruled out the boring "it's just an opening-print
artifact" explanation (only 3.6% of the effect sat in the first minute,
well under the 50% threshold that would have flagged that), so this is
a real, if unremarkable, everyday reversal pattern -- just not the
volume-graded version the mechanism doc set out to test.
Nothing was promoted. The candidate was pre-registered to close under
exactly this outcome (the doc's own falsifier condition (b), written
before any data was touched), and it did. This closes the "does trading
volume size predict reversal size" question for NQ daily bars for good,
adding to an earlier, separate closure (volume level alone, without
this reversal-gradient framing, was already ruled out in an earlier
hypothesis). Logged to the hypothesis ledger as hyp-000152 (REJECTED).
