## What was tested
One full pre-registered Discovery scan: does NQ tend to reverse
direction over the calendar month FOLLOWING a month that closed out
unusually weak (its final trading week down more than most months)? The
idea, from an academic paper: pension funds and cash-management accounts
have to sell stock every month-end to cover payroll and other fixed
obligations, which can temporarily push price down without any real
news behind it -- and if that's true, the pressure should let up and
price should drift back up over the following month.

## Result and why it matters
Close, but not a pass. Months that closed weak did tend to see a
slightly better next month (about 1.1% better than an average month),
and the pattern wasn't concentrated in just the first few days (which
would have meant it was really just an already-known, different effect
bleeding across the calendar boundary -- it wasn't). But the statistical
confidence interval came in at essentially touching zero on the low
end, meaning we can't rule out this being noise. With only 80 calendar
months of data to work with (NQ futures don't have a century of daily
history), this project doesn't have enough samples to call this pattern
proven or disproven -- only that it isn't visible clearly enough at the
data we have. This project's own rule treats a near-miss exactly like a
clean miss, so the idea closes rather than getting a second look with
the same data.
