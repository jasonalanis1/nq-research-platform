**A method bug caught and fixed inside the same cycle — worth recording.** The
first run ranked cells by their distance from the trigger-alone mean, and the
entire top of the table came back as `vxn_level_vs_trailing HIGH × <anything>
→ range`, |z| up to 8.5. That is not an interaction. It is the already-validated
"volatile days have bigger ranges" fact with a second state riding along, because
distance from the trigger-alone mean still contains the *context's* own main
effect. Fixed by ranking on the interaction proper — the cell's departure from
what the two states' separate main effects predict **additively**
(`z_vs_additive = (cell − (ctx_alone + trg_alone − all_mean)) / se`). The
shortlist collapsed from 1,495 cells to 172, and the whole character of the
top of the table changed. All three measures are still reported; only the
ranking changed.

## WHAT NEW INFORMATION THIS RUN PRODUCED

**Out of 14,740 interaction cells looked at, zero clear the multiplicity
yardstick.** With K = 14,740 looks, a single cell would need |z| ≈ 4.64 to stand
out family-wise. The strongest interaction on the entire Discovery slice, after
main effects are removed, is |z_vs_additive| = 4.4 — below it. 895 pairings were
dropped as circular before ranking; 4,405 cells fell below the n ≥ 100 floor.

What that means, stated carefully: at the resolution the project currently has
(11 states, tercile cuts, 8 windows, 5 outcomes), **the market states on disk do
not visibly modify one another beyond their own separate effects.** This is
descriptive, not a test — but it is directly relevant to the conditional-stack
format's prospects. Stack A was a powered null; the natural response was "we just
picked the wrong pair, source the next one from a look table." The look table now
exists, and it does not have an obviously better pair waiting in it. That is worth
knowing *before* spending the format's second and final powered null, not after.

Second, a methodological point that generalises beyond this table: ranking
cross-tab cells by distance from either marginal mean re-discovers the main
effects you already validated. Only the additive contrast isolates the
interaction. The project has a standing rule that the idea-factory queue is
descriptive and never a result; this run shows the queue can be *descriptively
wrong* too — pointing at restatements — if the contrast is chosen carelessly.
