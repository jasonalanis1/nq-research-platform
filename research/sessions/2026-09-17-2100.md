## Step 4 — S004 through SPECIFY → FREEZE → SCREEN

### The candidate, and why it is not H118

S004 is H118's VWAP-distance lineage rebuilt as a **strategy** and measured against **its own-drift
baseline**. H118 passed Discovery, Validation *and* Holdout and was rejected on September 12th because its
LOW-tercile 10-day drift is not distinguishable from NQ's own 10-day drift
(`research/studies/h118-baseline-diagnostic-2026-09-12.md`: LOW − ALL = +0.1162 R on Discovery, block-10

went up. H118's own spec, module and forward log were not touched.

### SPECIFY — `research/infrastructure/strategy-specs/S004-vwap-dist-low-drift-vs-own-drift.md`

Whole trade: signal session `P` has `vwap_dist_vs_atr(P)` in H118's **frozen Discovery LOW tercile**
(≤ −0.04604781358468863, hardcoded, never refit); **long 1 micro MNQ at the OPEN of the 09:30 ET bar of
session P+1** (not P's close — the descriptor is only known once P's last bar prints); exit at the OPEN of
the 09:30 bar of **session P+11**, exactly ten RTH sessions, as an absolute `market_context["exit_ts"]`
walked by choice 7; stop `entry − 4.0 × atr14`, a **disaster cap** (H118 had no stop); **no target**.

Mechanism (spec s.1): a close far below the session's own VWAP means the agency buyer working a multi-day
programme filled worse than the tape's own average and **still owes the quantity** — his benchmark clock
keeps running and the order is a commitment — while the seller who pushed price under VWAP has exhausted
willing supply. The forced flow in the following sessions is buying leftover quantity into a thinner offer.
The spec states the problem with that story in the same breath: **NQ drifts up over ten sessions anyway**,
so the mechanism must earn its keep on top of the market's default state. That is why the baseline is a
gate in s.5, not an appendix.

**S004 reads no reference data.** Entry, stop and exit are price-only. The validated VXN→next-session-range
sizing fact was **deliberately not used** for the stop, because it would make a price-only candidate depend
on a stale series and block its own screen (spec s.2). Verified in `tests/test_strategy_s004.py` against the
module's code with comments and docstrings stripped.

**Pre-freeze edge-vs-cost check (Amendment 2, `cost_model.specify_gate`), on the baseline-corrected edge and
not the raw statistic:** from the published Discovery numbers, LOW +83.26 net pt/trade vs ALL-days +67.57 in
the same units = **+15.693 pt/trade over NQ's own drift**, against **1.300 pt** MNQ market ⇒ **SCREEN**.
All four combinations in spec s.3 (1.300 / 1.050 / 0.745 / 0.495 pt), limit columns labelled OPTIMISTIC.

Where this should fail (spec s.6, its future Salvage menu): high-volatility regimes (a ten-session hold with
a 4 ATR cap is short volatility in disguise) · trending-down slices · time-of-day degenerate here · scheduled
news inside the window · heavy overlap of ten-session holds · the baseline itself.

### One methodology note on the screen harness

The cross-session walk in `bot_stack_paper_run._resolve_fill_outcome` hands the whole 2.2M-bar frame to
`.iterrows()` per trade — ~14,000 bars × 2,217 trades across the two arms. The screen uses a numpy resolver
with the **same decision rule** (stop tested first, then target, then the exit timestamp, so a stop wins a
same-bar tie; time exit booked at that bar's OPEN; an unresolvable trade returned as OPEN and excluded, never
force-closed). It is not trusted on that argument: **25 trades per arm were re-resolved with the original
function on the untrimmed frame — 0 disagreements**, and the screen refuses to print numbers if any appear
(`src/screen_s004_own_drift_baseline.py:_verify_against_original`).
