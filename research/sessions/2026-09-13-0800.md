## What was tested
Scan 027 tested M22's claim on ZN (10-year Treasury note futures):
whether ZN's price is systematically bid up over the final 2 trading
sessions of each calendar month, beyond what ZN normally does over any
random 2-session stretch. The theory: bond-index funds that must track
a published index's average maturity ("duration") are forced to buy
longer-dated futures at month-end when the index itself gets a bit
longer-dated from new government bond issuance -- a purely mechanical
rebalancing trade, concentrated into the last day or two of the month.
The test compared the actual last-2-session return (adjusted for how
volatile ZN typically is) against ZN's own typical 2-session move, using
82 month-end events from about 6.5 years of data.

## Result and why it matters
The point estimate pointed the right way (last-2-session returns
averaged noticeably higher than a random 2-session stretch), but the
statistical margin of error was too wide to call it real -- the range of
plausible values still included "no effect at all." It also wasn't a
last-minute settlement-price fluke (only about 10% of the move sat in
the final minute, well below the threshold that would flag an artifact).
In plain terms: the idea might still be true, but with only 82 month-end
events to look at, this test doesn't have enough statistical power to
either confirm or rule it out -- the same "thin sample, inconclusive"
situation the Treasury-auction test hit last week. Per this candidate's
own pre-written rule, a result like this closes the entry rather than
leaving it open-ended or re-testing with a looser bar.
A second, brand-new candidate was also fully written up this cycle but
not yet tested: whether NQ (the Nasdaq futures) tends to bounce back
over the following month after a weak final week of the current month --
the idea being that pension funds and payroll-funding accounts are
forced to sell stock for month-end cash needs regardless of what the
market is doing, temporarily pushing prices down in a way that unwinds
once that selling pressure passes. This is queued for a future draw once
