## What was tested
One full pre-registered Discovery scan: do NQ's returns tend to be
better than usual during the calendar week that contains the monthly
stock-options expiration (the third Friday of every month)? The idea:
banks and market-making firms that sold call options and are holding
short stock positions to hedge them have to buy back that stock as
those options run out and expire, creating steady buying pressure
across the week.

## Result and why it matters
No. Unlike this morning's month-end idea, which came agonizingly close,
this one just isn't there -- the confidence interval on the key
statistic spans from clearly negative to clearly positive, meaning the
data genuinely can't distinguish this pattern from noise, not a
near-miss. Interestingly, when broken down day by day, several
individual days within expiration week (Tuesday through Friday) DID
show statistically solid positive returns on their own -- but that's a
descriptive side-observation, not the actual test, and this project's
rule is clear: a scattered positive-looking detail next to a failed
main test doesn't count as a real finding. It closes.
Since that dropped the shelf below the 3-idea floor again, one new
idea was sourced: a different, well-known and much-studied pattern
called the "pre-holiday effect" -- markets have historically tended to
drift up on the trading day right before an exchange is closed for a
holiday (like the day before Thanksgiving or Christmas). This one's
mechanism story is honestly weaker than most of what's on the shelf
(no single named group forced to trade), but the pattern itself has
been observed across many different markets and many decades, which is
worth testing on its own. Written up and ready, not run yet.
