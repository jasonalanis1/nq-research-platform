## What was tested
The first sanctioned conditional retest under the new eight-condition protocol —
Jason's own question: does a pattern that failed on average (Level Sweep Reversal,

## Result and why it matters
No — for this pattern and this condition. Compressed-day trades averaged +0.03R
(R = profit measured in units of the risk taken), non-compressed days -0.065R. The
gap points the way the mechanism predicted, but the confidence interval on both
the compressed-day result and the gap spans zero comfortably. With 131 compressed-
day trades this is not a thin-sample excuse; the effect, if it exists, is smaller
than the noise. The methodology did exactly what it should: one condition chosen
before looking, one shot, honest answer, no second condition tried.
Shelf dropped to 2/3, so one entry was sourced: M29, the gap between what options
prices expect NQ to move (VXN) and what it has actually been moving. When that gap
is wide, options dealers are positioned in a way that dampens moves; when narrow or
negative, they amplify. It is the closest proxy in the data Tony already has for the
dealer flow Jason said he can't see. Filed as a state variable whose first use is a
descriptive look (once U11 exists), not a directional bet.

## What broke (this was a test run — reporting it plainly)
1. The device shell has a hard ~3-minute limit per command and does NOT keep
   background processes alive between commands. The scan's per-day loop
   (`scan_all_days`) re-materializes the whole minute index every day and takes
   >10 minutes on the full Discovery slice — it never finished on the first two
   attempts, and a background run silently died. Fixed inside the scan script by
   scanning one calendar year at a time (identical detection math, 4-day overlap
   for the prior-day level; 474 signals vs exp-023's 461 on the older data file —
   disclosed in the ledger). Lesson for every future per-trade scan: chunk by year.
2. `log_hypothesis()` call used wrong keyword names on the first try
   (`sample_size`/`mean_r` → `trade_count`/`expectancy_r`). Caught by the
   TypeError, no bad row written.
Neither affected a result. Both are now known to the successor prompt.
