## Why short horizons (per Standing Research Priority, set by Jason 2026-09-10)

Every prior scan (001/002/003) used HORIZONS = [1, 3, 5, 10] trading days,
close-to-close. A 10-day-hold candidate needs ~8 months of forward trades to
reach the 40-resolved-trade review point; a 1-day hold needs ~2 months. Same
statistical bar, faster answer. Scan 004 retargets the horizon set to the
short end: intraday, overnight, 1-day, 2-day. This does not lower the
promotion bar or retire multi-day work — it changes which candidates get
search budget going forward, per Jason's instruction.

Two honest costs of this choice, addressed directly in the constants below
rather than assumed away:
  1. Costs bite harder relative to a smaller expected move — the floor is
     NOT relaxed to compensate (see "Cost floor" below; if anything this
     scan adds a second, more direct absolute-cost check on top of the
     unchanged ATR floor).
  2. More researcher degrees of freedom exist intraday (many possible
     windows/anchors/thresholds) — every window/anchor/threshold below is
     fixed now, before this scan is written or run, not chosen after seeing
     which one "worked."

## Anchor convention (unchanged from v1/v2/v3, extended, not replaced)

Every state descriptor below is knowable as of day i's RTH OPEN (09:30 ET),
using only information from day i-1 and earlier, or calendar facts knowable
in advance — the exact "no lookahead" convention market_state_primitives.py
established. No descriptor here uses same-day information. This keeps ALL
THREE new descriptors on the same anchor as every existing descriptor, so
they can be scanned by the same engine and directly compared to Scan 001-003
candidates without inventing a second alignment convention.

The four horizons all measure FORWARD from that same day-i-open anchor point,
just at different windows of the price path that follows it (each fully in
the future relative to when the state is known):

  - `intraday`: Open[i] -> Close[i] (today's own RTH session return)
  - `overnight`: Close[i] -> Open[i+1] (the session-transition itself —
    tonight's overnight session, defined with the SAME 4:00 PM ET reference
    close / 09:30 ET open convention already frozen in study_overnight_gap.py
    (GAP_REFERENCE_HOUR/MINUTE, OPEN_HOUR/MINUTE) — reused unmodified, not
    re-derived)
  - `1d`: Close[i] -> Close[i+1] (same definition as Scan 001-003's h=1)
  - `2d`: Close[i] -> Close[i+2]

`intraday` and `overnight` are new outcome types this scan adds to the engine
(Scan 001-003 only ever measured close[i]->close[i+h]). `1d`/`2d` are kept
so every new descriptor can also be compared against the existing framework's
own horizon convention, not just the new short ones.

## The three new state descriptors (market_state_primitives_v4.py)

### 1. `days_to_monthly_opex` — options-expiration family

Trading days remaining from day i (inclusive of day i if day i itself is the
expiration date -> 0) to the next STANDARD MONTHLY equity-index options
expiration (3rd Friday of EVERY calendar month, all 12 months/year).

Explicitly NOT a resurrection of exp-035
(research/studies/futures-expiration-effects.md, REJECTED clean null,
src/study_futures_expiration.py): that test used the QUARTERLY IMM futures
rollover calendar (3rd Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec only, 4/year) as a
CONTINUOUS-CONTRACT-SPLICE proxy, applied to two specific pre-existing series
(IB Breakout's resolved trades, and overnight gap magnitude) via a binary
expiration-week/normal-week split. This descriptor uses the monthly cycle
(12/year, 3x the frequency), a different candidate mechanism (options
market-maker gamma hedging / pin risk into monthly expiration, not futures
contract-roll splicing), a continuous days-to-event state variable (not a
binary week flag), and is scanned across the general state-first
distribution engine (multiple outcome measures x multiple horizons x tercile
buckets), not a conditioning split on one pre-existing signal.

Bucketed into terciles over its observed range (~0 to ~21 trading days):
`low` = closest to the next expiration, `high` = furthest from it.

### 2. `prior_close_location_in_range` — session-transition family

(Close[i-1] - RTH_Low[i-1]) / (RTH_High[i-1] - RTH_Low[i-1]), i.e. where
YESTERDAY's RTH close sat within yesterday's own RTH high-low range (0 = at
the low, 1 = at the high). A fact about the completed prior day, known at
day i's open — same shift convention as every other v1-v3 descriptor.

Explicitly NOT a resurrection of hyp-000110 / closing-pressure-reversal
(REJECTED, cost-dominated, research/studies/closing-pressure-reversal-design-spec.md):
that test used last-15-minutes-of-RTH RETURN (magnitude/sign, a momentum
proxy for closing-auction imbalance) as a binary up/down/moderate split,
tested against ONE horizon (next-day close-to-close) with a directional
pre-commitment (reversal). This descriptor uses the close's LOCATION within
the full day's own range (a positional/structural fact, not a late-session
return), is bucketed continuously into terciles, and is scanned across all
four horizons above — most importantly `overnight`, the literal
session-transition outcome (does where RTH closed within its own range shape
the following overnight session specifically, not just next-day
continuation).

Bucketed into terciles over [0, 1]: `low` = closed near the day's low, `high`
= closed near the day's high.

### 3. `dist_to_multiday_reference_level_vs_atr` — multi-day reference-level family

min(|Open[i] - L| for L in {trailing 5-day high, trailing 5-day low,
trailing 20-day high, trailing 20-day low}) / ATR14[i], where each trailing
high/low is computed over the LOOKBACK days strictly before day i (i.e.
shifted, excludes day i itself — same convention as v1's
`location_in_range`, which already shifts its 20-day trailing high/low by
one day for the same reason).

Explicitly NOT a resurrection of the opening-hour / mid-morning
reference-level-fade studies (hyp-000081 family, exp-110/exp-112,
research/studies/opening-hour-reference-level-fade-spec.md,
midmorning-reference-level-fade-h83-spec.md): those tested INTRADAY touches
(first touch during a specific intraday window) of SINGLE-PRIOR-DAY levels
(prior-day high/low, overnight high/low, session VWAP) as fade ENTRY SIGNALS,
simulated as binary stop/target trades via backtest.simulate_trade. This
descriptor uses MULTI-DAY levels (5-day and 20-day trailing extremes, per
research/studies/project-audit-and-new-starting-point-2026-09-09.md's
explicit "multi-day (not intraday)" framing of what's untested), measures
PROXIMITY AT THE OPEN as a continuous ATR-normalized state variable (not an
intraday touch event triggering a signal), and is scanned across the general
state-first distribution engine, not a bespoke fade-trade simulation.

Bucketed into terciles: `low` = open is close to (effectively "touching") a
multi-day reference level, `high` = open sits in open space, far from any of
the four levels.

## Frozen scan constants (SAME as Scan 001-003 unless noted)

  - N_BOOTSTRAP = 3000, RANDOM_SEED = 7 (unchanged — same bootstrap CI
    convention as every prior scan, no reason to vary it)
  - MIN_N_FOR_RANKED = 40 (unchanged)
  - N_BUCKETS = 3 (tercile) per descriptor, same as Scan 003
  - ATR_NORMALIZED_FLOOR = 0.05 of avg daily ATR14, points (UNCHANGED
    absolute floor, applied identically regardless of horizon — using the
    same full-day ATR14 normalizer as before, NOT a horizon-scaled one, is
    itself a conservative choice for the intraday/overnight horizons: their
    natural move size is a fraction of a full day's range, so the same
    absolute-points floor is proportionally harder to clear than it was for
    the 10-day horizon it was originally set against. Per caveat 1
    (standing priority), this is left unchanged rather than loosened.)
  - NEW, additive, does not replace the above: `clears_absolute_cost_floor`
    = abs(effect_vs_baseline_pts) >= 3.0 x ROUND_TRIP_COST_POINTS (backtest.py;
    currently 1.0 pt round-trip -> 3.0 pt absolute floor). A cell must clear
    BOTH the existing ATR floor AND this absolute floor to be `ranked`. Added
    specifically because caveat 1 calls out that short-horizon costs bite
    harder in absolute terms than the ATR-normalized check alone might
    surface — disclosed as a new, pre-registered addition, not a retune of
    any existing candidate's result (nothing has been run yet).
  - HORIZONS = ["intraday", "overnight", "1d", "2d"] (see Anchor convention
    above for exact definitions)
  - STATE_VARS = ["days_to_monthly_opex", "prior_close_location_in_range",
    "dist_to_multiday_reference_level_vs_atr"]
