## Reading this round

The Observatory's core claim -- that it can produce well-defined,
plausible, disclosed candidates faster and with clearer mechanism
reasoning than intuition-driven search -- held up on process quality:
every candidate had a stated mechanism and a frozen, disclosed
measurement before any trade design existed. What it has NOT yet
demonstrated is translation into a profitable rule: 0 of 4 attempts
converted a measured behavior into a monetizable structure, including
one candidate (H81/H82) where two independently-designed exit
conventions were both tried against the same finding.

This round is too small (3 distinct findings, 1 tested twice) to
conclude the Observatory underperforms the historical approach on
final yield -- the historical approach's own 2-of-80 success rate took
~80 hypotheses to produce. It IS large enough to conclude the
measurement-first process has not, on its own, solved the translation
problem: knowing a behavior is real does not make it easy to monetize.

## Final verdict (round 1 complete, 4 findings / 5 hypotheses)

The regime-conditioning follow-up (H84 narrow vs. H85 wide, both
testing whether the open-bucket fade concentrates at one end of the
volatility spectrum) came back negative both ways -- neither narrow
nor wide regime turns the effect into a monetizable rule. Combined with
H81/H82/H83, this round is now a clean 0-for-5.

**Verdict: the Observatory, in this round, did not outperform the
historical intuition-driven approach on final yield (0/5 vs. 2/80 --
though 80 tries is a much larger base). Its real, demonstrated value in
this round was PROCESS quality, not yield**: every candidate arrived
with a disclosed mechanism and a frozen measurement before any trade
design existed, which made every failure interpretable (behavior vs.
design) rather than ambiguous. That is a genuine improvement over most
of the historical hypothesis set, which rarely stated a mechanism up
front.

**What this round does NOT establish:** that Observatory-sourced
candidates are systematically worse than intuition-sourced ones --
5 hypotheses is too small a sample to separate "this specific cluster
of reference-level-touch ideas didn't monetize" from "the Observatory
approach doesn't work." All 5 failures share one family (reference-
level touch-and-fade); the Observatory hasn't yet been asked to
generate a structurally different kind of candidate.

**Recommendation:** pause Observatory-sourced hypothesis generation on
this specific behavior family (reference-level touches) -- it has now
had 5 independent attempts across event/regime/time-bucket variations
and 2 different exit designs, more attention than any single idea has
received in this project's history, with a clean negative result.
Two paths forward, not a decision Claude is making unilaterally:
1. Run Observatory v1 again with a genuinely different event type
   (round numbers, prior swing highs/lows, session opens) to test
   whether the methodology does better on a structurally different
   behavior family, before judging the Observatory itself.
2. Redirect effort toward monetization-design methodology generally --
   this project's real bottleneck across BOTH the historical and
   Observatory-sourced hypotheses is converting a measured/intuited
   edge into a trade structure that survives cost, not generating
   candidate ideas.
Defaulting to path 1 (a differently-scoped Observatory re-scan) per the
standing autonomy instruction, since it's the more direct continuation
of the approved plan -- will report back once that scan and its top
candidate's result are in.
