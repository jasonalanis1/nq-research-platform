## Reading this round

The Observatory's core claim -- that it can produce well-defined,
plausible, disclosed candidates faster and with clearer mechanism
reasoning than intuition-driven search -- held up on process quality:
every candidate had a stated mechanism and a frozen, disclosed
measurement before any trade design existed. What it has NOT yet
demonstrated is translation into a profitable rule: 0 of 4 attempts
converted a measured behavior into a monetizable structure, including
one candidate (H81/H82) where two independently-designed exit
conventions were both tried against the same finding.

This round is too small (3 distinct findings, 1 tested twice) to
conclude the Observatory underperforms the historical approach on
final yield -- the historical approach's own 2-of-80 success rate took
~80 hypotheses to produce. It IS large enough to conclude the
measurement-first process has not, on its own, solved the translation
problem: knowing a behavior is real does not make it easy to monetize.

## Recommendation

Not a verdict yet -- one round (3 findings) is thin for a methodology
judgment. Two honest options, not a decision Claude is making unilaterally:
1. Run 1-2 more candidates from the remaining Observatory v1 output
   (Interesting-tier cells, or a second Observatory scan with different
   event types) to get to the full 3-5-candidate range Jason specified,
   before drawing a conclusion.
2. Treat this round's real finding as itself informative: the
   Observatory is good at producing well-specified BEHAVIORAL claims
   with disclosed provenance, but monetization design is the actual
   bottleneck in this project (consistent with the historical record --
   2 of 80 promoted-adjacent, 0 promoted) -- and redirect effort toward
   better systematic exit/monetization design methodology generally,
   rather than more Observatory scanning.

Proceeding with option 1 (1-2 more candidates) as the default per the
standing autonomy instruction, since it directly extends the plan
Jason already approved, unless he says otherwise.
